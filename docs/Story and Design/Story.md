# Story
Note this is the idea of the game. Not all needs to be implemented or used but all still exists as the concept and as 
inspiration.

## Story Overview

The game takes place both on the Internet and inside a Windows computer, where the player ultimately takes the role of 
an antivirus software. The story begins with the antivirus being downloaded by the computer's user, who is seeking help 
to save an infected system. However, before the antivirus can be installed, the player first experiences the journey as 
the courier responsible for delivering the software to its destination. After the antivirus has been installed, the 
player's objective becomes uncovering the source of the infection while defending the computer from increasingly 
dangerous malware. Along the way, the player explores the computer's file system, frees infected areas, defeats 
ransomware, and slowly uncovers the true cause of the virus outbreak. This journey eventually leads into the kernel of 
the operating system, where the player confronts the source of the corruption and frees the computer from the 
infestation.

The story can be divided into three unofficial acts:

- **Act I – The Internet:** The player begins as a courier traveling through the wilds of the Internet. This act focuses
on exploration and puzzles inspired by real networking concepts, such as DNS, routing, firewalls, and connection 
timeouts. The act concludes once the courier successfully reaches the destination computer.

- **Act II – The File System:** Now installed as the antivirus, the player explores the computer's directories, battles 
malware, helps the programs inhabiting the system, and gradually uncovers the origin of the infection.

- **Act III – The Kernel:** The final act takes place within the operating system itself. Here, both the enemies and 
puzzles become significantly more challenging as the player ventures into the heart of the computer to confront the core 
virus.

Throughout the game, the world should be built around fantasy interpretations of real computer concepts. Networking, 
operating systems, software, and malware are all represented as places, creatures, characters, and puzzles. Many 
references are intended as subtle jokes or puns that reward players with technical knowledge while remaining enjoyable 
and understandable to everyone else.

## Act I – The Internet
Act I introduce the player to the world, its themes, and its core philosophy. Rather than beginning as the antivirus, 
the player takes the role of a simple courier tasked with delivering a package through the vast wilderness known as the 
Internet. The package bears only a DNS address identifying its destination. To complete the delivery, the player must 
uncover where that address leads, navigate an unfamiliar world, and eventually find the computer for which the package 
was intended. Along the journey, the player encounters strange inhabitants, explores optional locations, and slowly 
discovers that the Internet is organized according to the rules of a computer network, translated into a medieval 
fantasy setting. The player is unable to fight during this act. Every obstacle must instead be overcome through 
exploration, observation, and puzzle solving. The Internet is intentionally dangerous and largely indifferent to the
player, reinforcing the feeling that they are merely a messenger passing through a much larger world. A timer runs 
throughout the entire act, representing the limited lifetime of the connection. If the player fails to deliver the 
package before time expires, the connection times out and the journey begins again from the start. This reset is not 
intended as a punishment but as the primary progression mechanic of the act. The world itself resets completely, but the
player's own knowledge does not. Each attempt reveals new information about the world, the route, and the puzzles that 
stand in the way. As the player learns, they naturally become faster, allowing them to venture further with each attempt 
until they eventually complete the delivery before the connection expires. The act encourages curiosity rather than 
efficiency. Many locations exist purely for exploration or world-building and contribute nothing to the main objective. 
The Dark Web, for example, is an entirely optional region filled with unusual characters and hidden secrets, rewarding 
players who choose to stray from the intended path. The act concludes once the player reaches the Router Village. There, 
the final pieces of information required to locate the destination computer are revealed, allowing the courier to 
complete the delivery and unknowingly initiate the installation of the antivirus. With the package delivered, the player
leaves behind the role of courier and begins the true adventure.
